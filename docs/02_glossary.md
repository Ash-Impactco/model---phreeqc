# Glossary

Plain meaning first, then why it matters for the Lomba da Maia trial. Numbers are placeholders unless said otherwise.

## The process

**ERW (enhanced rock weathering)**: speeding up the natural weathering of rock by grinding it and spreading it on land, so it captures CO2 faster.
*Why it matters:* it is what we are testing; the trial asks how fast it happens on this soil, not whether it works in principle.

**Cation**: a positively charged ion. For us: calcium (Ca2+), magnesium (Mg2+), sodium (Na+), potassium (K+).
*Why it matters:* cations released from the rock are what can carry carbon away as bicarbonate.

**Equivalent (eq)**: a unit of charge: one mole of a +1 ion, or half a mole of a +2 ion. 1 eq of cation can pair with 1 mol of bicarbonate, so 1 mol CO2.
*Why it matters:* lets us add Ca, Mg, Na and K together and convert straight to CO2 (44 g CO2 per eq). Our rock: 8.5 eq/kg.

**Bicarbonate (HCO3-)**: the form CO2 takes when dissolved in water that has been neutralised. It carries the captured carbon.
*Why it matters:* only cations that leave paired with bicarbonate count as carbon removal.

**pKa (pKa1 = 6.35)**: the pH at which half the dissolved CO2 is bicarbonate and half is still CO2.
*Why it matters:* our soil is about one unit below it, so only about 10% of cations go as bicarbonate.

**Non-carbonic-acid weathering**: rock dissolved by strong acids (from fertiliser, sulfur, organic acids) instead of carbonic acid. No CO2 is captured.
*Why it matters:* this is most of what happens in our acidic soil in the first years; registries deduct it.

**Downstream retention (0.86)**: the share of exported bicarbonate that stays stored after rivers and the ocean re-equilibrate. Some escapes as CO2 again.
*Why it matters:* trims the 372 kg/t potential to 320; Isometric may use its own site-specific value.

**Secondary minerals**: new clays and oxides that form in the soil and lock up some released cations.
*Why it matters:* 10% placeholder loss; mesocosm columns opened at the end will show how much.

## The soil

**CEC (cation exchange capacity)**: how many cations the soil can hold on the surfaces of clay and organic matter, in cmolc/kg.
*Why it matters:* sets how much released Ca and Mg the soil can store (25 cmolc/kg placeholder).

**Base saturation**: the share of the CEC taken by Ca, Mg, Na, K rather than acid (H, Al).
*Why it matters:* 30% placeholder; rises as rock dissolves, a farm benefit we can measure.

**Exchangeable vs total Ca + Mg**: exchangeable = held loosely on soil surfaces, available to plants (from a salt extraction). Total = everything, including what is still locked in rock grains (from fusion XRF).
*Why it matters:* exchangeable rising shows dissolution; total rising shows the rock is there. The total includes the exchangeable part, so a mass balance on total underestimates dissolution unless we subtract it.

**pH buffer capacity**: how much acid or base it takes to move soil pH by one unit, in cmolc/kg per pH.
*Why it matters:* volcanic soils buffer strongly, so pH moves slowly; it is unmeasured (5 placeholder) and needs a titration in December.

**Acid load**: the acid added each year by nitrogen fertiliser and grazing, after normal liming, in keq/ha/yr.
*Why it matters:* the rock's first job is to neutralise it, which removes no carbon. Comes from Luis's farm records.

**Andosol**: a soil formed on volcanic ash: light, high in organic matter and allophane, strongly buffered.
*Why it matters:* explains low bulk density (0.75), high buffering and possible high natural Ni, Cr and Zr.

## The rock

**PSD (particle size distribution)**: how the mass of the rock is spread across grain sizes, from sieving and laser diffraction.
*Why it matters:* the finest grains do most of the dissolving; it is the second biggest driver.

**BET**: a lab test of real surface area per gram, from gas adsorption.
*Why it matters:* checks the roughness factor and so the dissolution speed.

**Roughness (7)**: how much more real surface a grain has than a smooth sphere of the same size.
*Why it matters:* dissolution speed scales directly with it.

**XRF**: X-ray fluorescence, a lab test of the rock's (or soil's) bulk chemistry as oxides.
*Why it matters:* gives the 8.5 eq/kg and the 320 kg CO2/t ceiling.

**XRD**: X-ray diffraction, a lab test of which minerals are present and how much of each.
*Why it matters:* olivine and glass dissolve fast, feldspar and augite slowly; the mineral mix sets the timing.

**Glass**: volcanic rock that cooled too fast to form crystals.
*Why it matters:* dissolves relatively fast; its share and composition are uncertain in the placeholder.

**CIA / MIA (Chemical / Mafic Index of Alteration)**: ratios of aluminium (and iron) to the mobile cations; low values mean fresh, unweathered rock.
*Why it matters:* about 35 for our rock, confirming quarry fines are fresh and still hold their cations.

**Shrinking core**: the model's picture of a grain dissolving from the outside in at a steady speed, like a sucked sweet.
*Why it matters:* it is why small grains vanish fast and big grains barely change in 30 months.

**Field factor (0.2 / 1 / 3)**: how fast rock weathers in a real field compared with a lab beaker. Covers patchy wetting, poor contact, coatings.
*Why it matters:* the biggest single uncertainty (rank correlation 0.83); mesocosms and the first field rounds calibrate it.

## Measuring it

**Immobile tracer**: an element (Ti, Zr) that is rich in the rock, does not dissolve, and so tells you how much rock is in a soil sample.
*Why it matters:* needed for the classic mass balance; ours do not work (Ti contrast too small, soil Zr higher than rock).

**Tau (dissolved fraction)**: the share of the rock's cations that have dissolved, worked out from soil samples.
*Why it matters:* the direct proof of weathering; expected about 0.07 at 30 months, detectable only above about 0.17.

**Mass balance**: comparing cations in the soil before and after spreading, correcting for how much rock was added, to infer how much dissolved.
*Why it matters:* the textbook method; too noisy for us in 30 months, useful later.

**Lysimeter**: a ceramic cup buried in the soil (here 30 cm) that sucks out soil water for analysis.
*Why it matters:* our best field measure of weathering in the grant period (+0.20 meq/L at 80 t/ha).

**Mesocosm**: a soil column in the lab, here 15 cm wide, 30 cm of trial soil, watered weekly for 12 months.
*Why it matters:* the only place we can measure the dissolution and the bicarbonate split cleanly, and calibrate the field factor.

**LOQ (limit of quantification)**: the smallest concentration a lab can measure reliably.
*Why it matters:* alkalinity LOQ about 0.05 meq/L; 20 t/ha lysimeter signal (0.03) is below it, 80 t/ha (0.20) above.

## Statistics

**RCBD and blocks (randomised complete block design)**: plots grouped into blocks of similar ground; every rate appears once in each block.
*Why it matters:* the slope and elevation gradient is absorbed by the blocks, so it does not swamp the rate effect.

**Randomisation**: assigning rates to plots within a block by chance (seed 2026), redrawn if rate lines up with slope.
*Why it matters:* stops us, or the hill, from biasing the result.

**Power**: the chance the trial detects an effect that is really there (we aim for 80% or more).
*Why it matters:* drives 12 vs 16 plots; 16 plots gives about 0.95 at medium variability, if the central chemistry is right.

**Regression slope**: the change in a response per t/ha of rock, fitted across all rates.
*Why it matters:* our main result; uses all 16 plots at once, so it is much stronger than comparing one rate to the control.

**MANOVA**: a test of several responses together (pH, exchangeable and total Ca + Mg).
*Why it matters:* a joint check that the rock moved the soil at all.

**Mixed model**: a regression that uses all six sampling rounds and knows the same plot is measured repeatedly.
*Why it matters:* asks whether the rate effect grows over time; a fallback if variability is high.

**Holm correction**: a rule that makes p-values stricter when you run several comparisons, so you do not find effects by luck.
*Why it matters:* why "40 t/ha vs control" is weak (power about 0.35 even with 16 plots).

**Monte Carlo**: running the model hundreds of times with uncertain inputs drawn at random, to see the spread of outcomes.
*Why it matters:* gives our ranges; it varies 9 inputs, so the true spread is wider.

**P10 / P50 / P90**: the values below which 10%, 50% and 90% of the Monte Carlo runs fall.
*Why it matters:* P50 is the middle case; P10 to P90 is the "likely range" we quote.

**Spearman rank correlation**: a number from -1 to +1 saying how strongly an input pushes the output up or down, using ranks.
*Why it matters:* tells us which measurements to buy first (field factor 0.83, pH 0.35, fines 0.31).

## Rules and money

**DNSH (Do No Significant Harm)**: the EU rule that a funded project must not damage soil, water, biodiversity and so on.
*Why it matters:* the metals question and the stream monitoring feed our DNSH answers in the application.

**Isometric**: a carbon registry with an ERW protocol, used here as the reference for how carbon would be counted.
*Why it matters:* its rules on deductions, downstream losses and non-carbonic-acid weathering shape how we describe carbon; the trial will not issue credits.
